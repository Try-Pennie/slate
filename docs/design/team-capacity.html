Team capacity

The settled design. Every frame is drawn inside the product's real chrome, captured from Slate's /admin/team-capacity page signed in as the seeded admin, captured for the 24 design rounds Dan settled on 2026-10-19 for Try-Pennie/slate#3934 (approved by Sam, the marketing lead it is for), and recovered from that round's settled file for #3954 on 2026-10-20, from the committed fixture the built page renders too. The build is done when it scores 100% against this file on both halves.

Decisions

  1. D1 Its own admin page named Team capacity, last under Availability. Its own admin page, named Team capacity, last under Availability in the sidebar, beside Team hours. Managers and admins can open it. Chosen over a section of Bucket availability; the name pairs with Team hours and says it is the whole team's room. Requirement 1. Checked on f1: main h1 reads Team capacity, and the sidebar's current item is Team capacity, after Bucket availability.
  2. D2 The team total per day leads, advisors below. The team total per day leads the page, advisors below. Meta spend cannot be aimed at a debt tier, since the tier is known only after the form, so the team number is the one Sam can act on. Requirement 2. Checked on f1: the first day card comes before the first advisor row.
  3. D3 Full means bookable time used up, counted as calls that still fit. Full means bookable time used up. Calls open counts how many more 20 minute calls still fit under the booker's own rules (the 10 minute gap each side, notice, working hours, office hours), not open start times, which overlap. A cal.com call, a block and a lunch on the calendar all count as taken, because the calendar copy cannot tell them apart. Requirement 14. Checked on f1, f2, f3, f4, f5, f6, f7, f8, f9: each card's calls open is the fixture's open calls for that day, summed over the advisors who are counted.
  4. D4 The cards follow the configured booking window; today counts time ahead. The four cards follow the configured booking window: changing offered days to 3 or 7 changes the page. Today counts only time still ahead. Once every shift today has ended, its card says the day is done rather than that nobody could be counted (D42, Dan, 2026-10-22, #4127). Requirement 2. Checked on f1: there is one day card per offered day of the booking window.
  5. D5 Card layout: calls open, bar, % full, the pace, advisors off, calendars not syncing. Checked on f1: a card holds calls open, its bar, its percent, its pace lines and its time off line, in that order, and the time off line reads N advisors off, or 1 advisor off.
  6. D6 The percent rounds down. The percent rounds down, so it never reads 100 while a call is still open. Requirement 2. Checked on f1, f2, f3, f4, f5, f6, f7, f8, f9: each card's percent is the fixture's share of capacity used that day, rounded down.

It began as Dan's "the darker color should indicate time taken"; on light it is literally darker, and in the dark theme, where the fill is the brighter sky, the rule holds as filled versus empty (Dan, 2026-10-19). Idioms of the medium, to translate rather than copy

  • Every frame is drawn inside Slate's real admin page, captured from the running app (header, sidebar, page title card), and uses Slate's real controls: the month calendar is the CalendarMonth component rendered as it ships, inside the same panel DateTimeField opens, and the input and buttons use inputCls and the pn-btn classes. What remains an idiom of drawing a design as a static page: the invented advisor names; the snapshot itself, which in the product is read live; the later day and the past day, which show Thursday's numbers under another date because nothing was stored for them when this was drawn; the full day, drawn by emptying today; today after every shift has ended, drawn by running today's shifts out, with its saved history (58 calls unbooked of 690) invented; the day nobody works, drawn by emptying Wednesday; the booking pace readings, written into the fixture by hand rather than measured, and the pace that can't load or is out of date, both pretended; the calendar not syncing and the hour off, both pretended; the seeded admin in the header (AD); and the calendar links, which go nowhere here. Frames are 1280 pixels wide, a laptop.
